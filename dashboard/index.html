How the values are priced

The comps curve

Category adjustments

Each category's median market-cap-per-TVL moves its predictions, shrunk toward no adjustment when the category has few priced peers. Categories with fewer than three peers get no adjustment at all.

Growth and tenure multiply the fitted value: seven-day TVL change, clamped to −50%…+100% at half weight, and a maturity term rising from 70% at listing to 100% over three years. The blind test above scores the whole predictor — curve, category adjustment, growth, maturity and the 0.02×–1.50× multiple clamp — on five folds the fit never saw. A smaller multiple means the protocol's TVL is large next to what comparable tokens are worth; the clamp keeps one outlier curve from pricing any protocol above 1.50× its TVL or below 0.02×.

How the screen works: a protocol is ranked only if the snapshot records no token for it and it passes the watchlist rule's checks on the snapshot's own fields — not a bridge, liquid-staking or exchange product, no version marker in its name. Above the $50M floor the watchlist itself is the rule's verdict and is followed exactly, because the full rule also sees the parent-protocol field this snapshot does not carry; below the floor the same screen on the snapshot's fields decides. Everything set aside is listed with its reason, and everything that records a token is listed separately.

Reading a row: implied value is the model's answer to "what would a token be worth?"; the multiple is that value divided by TVL; the verdict compares the value with the cost of a standard farm — FARM is the top third of that efficiency, THIN the middle, SKIP the bottom third, where the plan spends nothing and names the trigger that would reopen the protocol.

Values are model estimates from one frozen snapshot, not predictions. "Records no token" means the snapshot's token fields are empty; it is not a claim that a token will launch. Nothing here is financial advice.
